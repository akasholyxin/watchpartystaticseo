**Short answer:** Netflix and most streaming services protect their video, so a screen share in Discord, Zoom or Google Meet usually shows your friends a **black screen with sound**. That's by design. The fix is not to share your screen at all: let everyone play the video on their own account and keep it in sync with a watch party extension. Everyone gets the full picture, at full quality.

## Why the screen goes black

Streaming services use video protection so their films can't be captured. Screen sharing is a form of capture, so the protected picture is left out of what gets sent and your friends see black. It isn't a setting you've got wrong in Discord or Zoom.

We don't recommend workarounds that defeat that protection. Besides breaking the service's rules, they still leave you with the second problem: screen shares look bad.

## Screen sharing vs a watch party

| | Screen sharing | Watch party (sync) |
|---|---|---|
| Netflix and other protected services | Usually a black screen | Works |
| Picture quality | Compressed, often blurry | Full quality for each person |
| Smoothness | Stutters, low frame rate | Smooth |
| Host's internet | Heavy upload | Light |
| Who needs access to the video | Only the host | Everyone |
| Talking | The call app | Chat, reactions and video calls built in |

## Why screen sharing struggles with movies, even when it works

1. **It's a copy of a copy.** Your computer captures the screen and re-encodes it, losing detail.
2. **Upload is the bottleneck.** Home connections upload far slower than they download.
3. **Audio drift.** Sound and picture can fall out of step for the people watching.
4. **One person carries everything.** If the host's connection hiccups, everyone's movie does too.

## How to watch together without screen sharing

1. Everyone adds a watch party extension; {{brand}} is free for desktop Chrome.
2. Everyone opens the same title on their **own account**.
3. The host creates a party and shares the room code.
4. Press play. Play, pause and seek stay in step for everyone, and you can talk on the built-in video call (or stay in Discord).

## When screen sharing is fine

- Showing a short clip that isn't from a protected service
- Something only one person can access, where the service allows it
- Showing something that isn't a video at all
